Step 1 · Get started

Why is summer sunlight stronger than winter sunlight?

The Sun doesn't get much closer to us in summer. What changes is the angle the light arrives at. In this experiment you'll tilt a databot's light sensor away from a light and measure how the brightness changes, in lux.

Try it first. Grab the databot and tilt it, or press Play. Watch how much of the beam still lands on the sensor.

About the sensor

The databot has an ambient light sensor on its top face. It reports illuminance in lux: how much light falls on each square metre of surface. A sunny day outside is about 30,000–100,000 lux. A bright desk lamp up close is a few thousand. A dim room is around 100.

Lux depends on the surface, not just the light. Tilt the surface and the same light spreads over more area, so each square metre gets less.

Step 4 · Collect

Measure the light at three angles

  1. Lay the databot flat, facing the light. Press Start recording.
  2. Hold it still for about 15 seconds. Then tilt it halfway (about 45°) and press Tilted halfway.
  3. Hold it still for about 15 seconds. Then tilt it almost sideways (about 75°) and press Almost sideways.
  4. Hold it for about 15 more seconds, then press Stop recording.

Or press Autoplay experiment to watch the practice databot do all of this by itself while you take notes.
